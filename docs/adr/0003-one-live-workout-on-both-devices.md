# One Live Workout on both devices, with no owner

The product exists to fix other apps' clumsy "start it on the Watch or the iPhone" split, so the user never chooses, starts, stops or moves a Workout between devices. A Live Workout is therefore one Workout present on both iPhone and Watch, and either device can log, edit, Finish or Discard it at any time, Connected or Disconnected. There is no owning or "host" device and no handover. Each device keeps its own copy and they exchange small, ID-carrying changes: live over the HealthKit mirroring channel with acknowledgements, held in an outbox and resent when Disconnected, with endings also carried by a guaranteed queue (ADR-0007). So every Set is still created on exactly one device (ADR-0001), and the Watch still syncs only through the iPhone (ADR-0002). We rejected a single owning device with explicit or automatic handover because it forces "go do that on the other device" moments. We rejected the Watch syncing through iCloud as well, because iCloud is too slow for a Live Workout and would add a second writer.

## Consequences

- **The Watch joins automatically.** As far as the platform allows, it joins with zero taps: the iPhone app brings it in (`startWatchApp`) whenever the iPhone app is running, and the Watch app starts its session the moment it's opened during a Live Workout. On every supported OS, if the Watch comes back while the iPhone is locked, it joins when either app is next used. The heart-rate gap this leaves is accepted, and there is no version-specific path.
- **Finish is final wherever it is tapped**, unless the other device ended the Workout differently while they were apart; then the Workout is marked Needs review and the user chooses on the iPhone (ADR-0007). The Finish carries its tap time, which is the Workout's end.
- **One Health record, written at once and improved later.** The Watch writes the Health record when it is in the Workout and hears of the Finish. Otherwise the iPhone immediately writes a plain record (no heart rate), and a Watch that later returns with heart-rate data writes the full record ending at the Finish time while the iPhone deletes its own plain one.
- Merge rules for concurrent changes belong to the reconciliation design, not to this ADR.
