# Splitting one Watch session into two Workouts, and how the mirroring channel behaves on disconnect

This note tests a proposed rule against the platform: *if device A ends the Workout (Finish or Discard) at time T while Disconnected, and device B logged Sets after T, then on reconnect those later Sets split off into a new, separate Finished Workout.* So one continuous Watch session (e.g. 18:00–18:50) may have to become **two** `HKWorkout`s in Health (18:00–18:40 and 18:40–18:50), or, after a Discard, only the later part is kept. It also covers what the HealthKit mirroring data channel does across a disconnect, and how far the two devices' clocks can be trusted.

Targets iOS 17 / watchOS 10 minimum. Terms follow `CONTEXT.md` (**Workout**, **Set**, **Live**, **Finished**, **Discard**, **Connected** / **Disconnected**). It builds on and does not repeat:
- [`watch-workout-and-sync.md`](watch-workout-and-sync.md): session lifecycle, mirroring set-up, reconnect with a new session instance, WatchConnectivity.
- [`watch-force-quit-and-hr-samples.md`](watch-force-quit-and-hr-samples.md): samples are saved *during* the session, `discardWorkout` keeps them, past end dates.
- [`automatic-watch-join.md`](automatic-watch-join.md) §4: `stopActivity(with:)` / `endCollection(withEnd:)` accept past dates.

Sources were read on 2026-09-23. Header quotes come from the iOS 26.0 SDK headers (iOS 17.0 SDK where noted), read through the [xybp888/iOS-SDKs](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs) mirror. The text is Apple's, but the mirror is not an Apple site. Every claim is labelled **Apple**, **Developer report** (non-Apple forum posts or blogs) or **Inference / unverified**.

## Answer

**Facts**
1. **One session and one live builder make at most one `HKWorkout`.** A stopped session "cannot be reused", `endCollection` "deactivates the workout builder", and nothing can be added after `finishWorkout`. The builder *can* be given an end date in the past (already established). But anything the Watch recorded after T has already been saved to Health as loose samples, whatever end date the builder gets.
2. **A second `HKWorkout` for any past window is supported.** Use a plain `HKWorkoutBuilder` on iOS 12+ or watchOS 5+: "Samples … may be added to a builder … to create a workout that occurred in the past." `add(_:)` accepts samples that are **already saved** ("The samples will be saved to the database if they have not already been saved"), so the existing heart-rate and energy samples can be associated with it without copying. The `HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier` header says a replacement sample "will be added to **all HKWorkouts** … containing the replaced sample", which implies a sample *can* belong to more than one workout. Apple never states this directly, and nothing says what the live builder already associated with the first workout (**unverified, test on a device**).
3. **Deleting by time window works only for objects our app saved.** `deleteObjects(of:predicate:)` "Deletes objects saved by this application that match the provided type and predicate", and a batch is all-or-nothing. Apple does **not document** whether deleting an `HKWorkout` deletes its associated samples. One developer report says energy and distance went with it. An Apple engineer said that **Exercise minutes awarded for a workout saved on iPhone cannot be deleted by the app**. The Watch app and the iPhone app are **separate HealthKit sources with separate permissions** (developer reports), so the iPhone probably cannot delete samples the Watch app wrote (**unverified**).
4. **The mirroring channel does not queue across a disconnect.** The header says `sendToRemoteWorkoutSession` sends "to the **connected** remote workout session" and returns an error "if the session is not mirroring". The iPhone's mirrored session is invalid after `didDisconnectFromRemoteDeviceWithError`, and a new instance arrives only "if the primary workout session is still running". **Size limit (Apple, in the header since the iOS 17.0 SDK): 100 KB in any 10-second window**, which corrects "no size limit documented" in `watch-workout-and-sync.md`. Receive-side ordering is documented only for data cached while the receiver is **suspended**: it arrives in the order the local system received it, possibly minutes late. No end-to-end delivery or ordering guarantee is documented. Developer reports describe sends that hang forever in about 5% of Workouts, and errors "Primary session is unreachable" (554) and "Failed to send data to remote session" (100).
5. **If the Watch ends the session while Disconnected, HealthKit never tells the iPhone.** "When a workout session is ended, mirroring is automatically stopped", and reconnection is attempted only while the primary session is still running. The iPhone learns only through our own channel (for example WatchConnectivity `transferUserInfo`) or by seeing the Watch's `HKWorkout` arrive through Health sync, which has no documented latency.
6. **Clocks:** no Apple developer document gives the Watch's time source or the expected iPhone–Watch skew. Community answers (not Apple staff) say the Watch "inherits its time directly from the paired iPhone", which sets its own from Apple's NTP-backed time servers. The Watch's "+N min" face offset changes only the displayed time (Apple). Skew is **unknown**, so plan for seconds, not milliseconds (**unverified**).

**Recommendation (ours, not Apple's)**
- **The split rule is feasible in Health, but only as a *post-hoc rebuild*, not by bending the live session.** Let the Watch's live session run to its real end and finish it normally, with `HKMetadataKeyExternalUUID` = Workout 1 and `SyncIdentifier` = Workout 1 at v1. When the split is detected: (a) **on the Watch** (the source that owns the samples), rebuild Workout 1 as 18:00–T with a plain `HKWorkoutBuilder`, the same sync identifier and v2, and add the already-saved samples in [start, T]. (b) Build Workout 2 (T–end) the same way, with its own identifiers, adding the samples in [T, end]. (c) In the Discard case, skip (a) and delete Workout 1's samples in [start, T] by source and date predicate. **Everything in (a)–(c) turns on points that are unverified here and must be tested on a device before the rule is committed to:** whether re-saving with a higher sync version really replaces a workout, what happens to the samples associated with the old version, and whether a sample can sit in two workouts.
- **Do the Health rebuild on the Watch, or accept a weaker iPhone-only result.** Because the sources are separate, an iPhone-side rebuild cannot delete or replace what the Watch app wrote (**unverified, but likely**).
- **Don't use the mirroring channel as the only path for anything that must arrive.** Treat it as a best-effort live path, capped at 100 KB per 10 s, with no queueing while Disconnected. Carry every change as an idempotent ID-keyed record that is **also** written to a durable queue (`transferUserInfo`), and run a full-state catch-up exchange each time a new mirrored session arrives. Finish and Discard especially must go through the durable path, because a Watch-side end while Disconnected never reaches the iPhone through HealthKit.
- **Don't rely on cross-device clock comparison at second-level precision to decide which Sets fall after T.** Prefer ordering by what each device had *seen* (for example "B had not yet received A's Finish when it logged this Set"). Where timestamps must be compared, estimate the offset on every reconnect by exchanging timestamps over the channel *(design inference)*.

## 1. One session → one `HKWorkout`; past end dates

- **Apple (header, `stopActivityWithDate:`):** "Once a workout session is stopped, it cannot be reused to start a new workout session. Sensor algorithms will be stopped and no new data will be generated for this session." `end`: "Once a workout session is ended, it cannot be reused to start a new workout session." [HKWorkoutSession.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKWorkoutSession.h)
- **Apple (header, `HKWorkoutBuilder`):** `endCollectionWithEndDate:` "Sets the workout end date and deactivates the workout builer." `addSamples:`: "It is an error to call this method after finishWorkoutWithCompletion: has been called." `finishWorkout`: "Creates and saves an HKWorkout using samples and events that have been added to workout previously." [HKWorkoutBuilder.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKWorkoutBuilder.h). Together these mean one builder produces one workout. No API splits a finished builder.
- **Apple (WWDC18 707):** after `endCollection` with the workout's end date, "no data is going to be collected for this workout", and `finishWorkout` saves it "with all of its associated data". [WWDC18 707 transcript (third-party copy)](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2018/707/)
- Past end date: allowed, but whether samples after it stay *associated* is unverified. See [`automatic-watch-join.md` §4](automatic-watch-join.md) and [`watch-force-quit-and-hr-samples.md` §3](watch-force-quit-and-hr-samples.md). Samples after T are already in Health either way, because they are saved while the session runs ([Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions)).
- **One session at a time:** a new primary session fails, or ends the current one, with `HKErrorAnotherWorkoutSessionStarted` ("Another primary workout session has started or is already ongoing by this or another application"). [HKDefines.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKDefines.h). So "end session 1 at T and immediately run session 2 from T" cannot overlap. A new session that starts *now* with `startActivity(with: T)` would be the retroactive session Apple warns against ("don't try to retroactively create a workout session on Apple Watch", [Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions)). Its live data source is also not documented to pick up samples from before the session existed (**unverified**).
- **Activities are not a split.** `beginNewActivity(configuration:date:metadata:)` / `endCurrentActivity(on:)` (watchOS 9+) divide *one* session into activities, but the result is still one `HKWorkout`. [HKWorkoutSession.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKWorkoutSession.h)
- **Stopping collection early (live).** `HKLiveWorkoutDataSource.disableCollection(for:)` "Removes the specified quantity type from the types to collect". `enableCollection(for:predicate:)`: "If non-nil, collected samples must match this predicate." [HKLiveWorkoutDataSource.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKLiveWorkoutDataSource.h). *Inference:* a date predicate might stop the builder associating samples after T, but the sensors still save them to Health, so this does not remove them.

## 2. A second `HKWorkout` for a past window, from existing samples

- **Apple (header, class comment):** "Samples, events, and metadata may be added to a builder either during a live workout session or to create a workout that occurred in the past. Calling finishWorkoutWithCompletion: will create a new workout with samples, events, and metadata that have been provided." `initWithHealthStore:configuration:device:` "Creates a new HKWorkoutBuilder unconnected to any HKWorkoutSession or any sources of data." Available iOS 12 / watchOS 5. [HKWorkoutBuilder.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKWorkoutBuilder.h)
- **Apple (docs):** "For watchOS, use an HKWorkoutSession and an HKLiveWorkoutBuilder instead." This is guidance, not an availability restriction. [HKWorkoutBuilder](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutbuilder)
- **Existing samples can be added (Apple, header, `addSamples:`):** "The samples will be saved to the database **if they have not already been saved**. The constraints of -[HKHealthStore saveObject:withCompletion:] apply to this method as well. The start date of the samples must be later than the start date of the receiver." So already-saved samples are associated, not copied. The save constraints mean we need **share** authorisation for heart rate and active energy. The old API `addSamples:toWorkout:` (deprecated in iOS 17 / watchOS 10 in favour of `HKWorkoutBuilder`) worked the same way: "These samples will then be returned by a query that contains this workout as a predicate. If a sample is added that is not saved yet, then it will be saved for you." [HKHealthStore.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKHealthStore.h), [add(_:to:completion:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/add(_:to:completion:))
- **Developer report:** adding samples queried with an `HKAnchoredObjectQuery` (system-generated energy) into a live builder with `builder.add` was suggested as a workaround. The other poster got "unauthorized" when they had requested only read access for that type. [Forum 110498](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/110498)
- **Many-to-many (Apple, indirect):** the `HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier` header says "The new sample will be added to **all HKWorkouts** or HKCorrelations containing the replaced sample." [HKMetadata.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKMetadata.h). This is the only first-party text that implies a sample can belong to several workouts. No page states it outright, and nothing says whether the Health app or Activity counts such a sample once or twice (**unverified**).
- **Duplicates:** associating an existing sample does not create a new one (header above). Duplicates arise only if we *create* new samples with the same values. Avoid that, because a workout "needs a set of associated samples that add up to the summary's total" and the Move ring counts active-energy samples. [HKWorkout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkout), [Adding samples to a workout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/adding-samples-to-a-workout)
- **Replacing Workout 1 with a shorter version.** Apple: "When you save an HKObject with a sync identifier, the system looks for any existing objects with the same sync identifier … If the new object has a greater sync version, the system replaces the old object with the new one." [HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkmetadatakeysyncidentifier). Also: "set by its source" [HKMetadata.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKMetadata.h). **Unverified:** (a) whether replacement works *across sources* (iPhone app replacing a Watch-app workout; see §3), and (b) whether the samples associated with the old workout carry over or must be added again. *Inference:* add them again explicitly in the rebuild.
- **Activity rings (Apple):** on iOS, "Workout objects automatically contribute to both the Move and Exercise rings … The Exercise ring increases by the workout's total duration." On watchOS the Exercise ring comes from the session's sensor data. [HKWorkout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkout). *Inference:* a rebuilt workout saved from the iPhone could add Exercise minutes on top of those the Watch session already earned (**unverified**).

## 3. Deleting samples in a time window; deleting a workout

- **Apple:** `deleteObjects(of:predicate:withCompletion:)` "Deletes objects saved by this application that match the provided type and predicate. Your app can delete only those objects that it has previously saved … If the user revokes sharing permission for an object type, you can no longer delete those objects … When deleting multiple objects, if any object cannot be deleted, none of them are deleted." [deleteObjects(of:predicate:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/deleteobjects(of:predicate:withcompletion:)), [HKHealthStore.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKHealthStore.h)
- **Apple (WWDC20 10184):** "When deleting a sample, you need to ensure that it is actually a sample that was previously written by your app … A good best practice here is to first query for the sample and then delete it." [WWDC20 10184](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10184/). Suggested predicate: `predicateForSamples(withStart: T, end: end)` combined with `predicateForObjects(from: HKSource.default())` *(inference)*.
- **Are session samples "saved by this application"?** Apple says the Watch "saves samples to the HealthKit store" while the session runs ([Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions)). It does not say which `HKSource` they carry. **Unverified:** that live heart-rate and energy samples carry *our Watch app* as source, which is required for deletion. Test on a device.
- **Watch app vs iPhone app are separate sources (Developer reports):** since single-target Watch apps (Xcode 14), the iPhone and Watch apps show "separate permission entries", and "it's like we have 2 different app reading and writing in 2 different HealthKit stores". [Forum 715238](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/715238). *Inference:* only the Watch app can delete or replace what the Watch app wrote.
- **Deleting a workout: associated samples not documented.** Apple's delete pages say nothing about a cascade ([delete(_:withCompletion:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/delete(_:withcompletion:)-78l1m)). Developer report (Jun 2021): deleting only the `HKWorkout` removed Active Energy and Distance but not Exercise minutes. **Apple Frameworks Engineer:** "Exercise minutes for workouts saved on phone are awarded by the system and you cannot delete those. The user has to manually delete them in the Health app." [Forum 681755](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/681755). *Consequence:* a Discard, or a rebuild done from the iPhone, cannot fully undo ring effects. Delete samples explicitly rather than relying on a cascade.
- Deleted objects appear as `HKDeletedObject` to anchored queries, but only temporarily. [delete(_:withCompletion:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/delete(_:withcompletion:)-17hzm)

## 4. Mirroring channel on disconnect and reconnect

### Documented behaviour
- **Sending (Apple, header, unchanged since the iOS 17.0 SDK):** "Sends an NSData object to the **connected** remote workout session … It's only intended to be used for data that describes the current state of the workout, such as accumulated metrics, and any data needed to keep your app on both devices in sync. **The maximum amount of data that can be sent is 100 KB in any given 10-second time window.** If this limit is exceeded, an error will be returned in the completion handler. **An error will also be returned if the session is not mirroring.**" [HKWorkoutSession.h (26.0)](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKWorkoutSession.h), [HKWorkoutSession.h (17.0)](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS17.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKWorkoutSession.h). The web doc page omits the limit. [sendToRemoteWorkoutSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutsession/sendtoremoteworkoutsession(data:completion:))
- **Receiving (Apple):** "When suspended, HealthKit gathers the data coming from the remote session. When the app resumes, HealthKit sends an array containing all the data objects it has accumulated … **The data objects in the array appear in the order that the local system received them.** During the workout session, HealthKit wakes the iOS app periodically to deliver any cached data objects, but **there might be several minutes between delegate calls**." watchOS behaves the same while the app is suspended. [didReceiveDataFromRemoteWorkoutSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutsessiondelegate/workoutsession(_:didreceivedatafromremoteworkoutsession:))
- **Disconnect (Apple):** "After the system calls this method, the provided workout session is no longer valid, and you can no longer use it … If the primary workout session is still running, it automatically tries to reconnect. If successful, the companion iOS device calls the workoutSessionMirroringStartHandler block again, passing in a new, valid HKWorkoutSession instance." [didDisconnectFromRemoteDeviceWithError](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutsessiondelegate/workoutsession(_:diddisconnectfromremotedevicewitherror:)), [workoutSessionMirroringStartHandler](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/workoutsessionmirroringstarthandler)
- **Watch ends while Disconnected (Apple):** "When a workout session is ended, mirroring is automatically stopped." [stopMirroringToCompanionDevice](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKWorkoutSession.h). Combined with "reconnects only if the primary is still running", *inference:* the iPhone gets no further HealthKit callback. It has already had `didDisconnect` and is holding an invalid session.
- **Late state callbacks (Apple):** `didChangeToState:fromState:date:` "If your application is suspended then the delegate will receive this call once the application resumes, which may be much later than when the original state change ocurred." [HKWorkoutSession.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKWorkoutSession.h)

### Not documented, or reported by developers
- **Messages sent while Disconnected: not queued (inference from the header), not documented.** The iPhone has no valid session to send on. On the Watch, the primary session is "not mirroring" while it is reconnecting, so a send should error. Nothing says whether HealthKit buffers and replays Watch→iPhone sends across a reconnect. **Assume dropped.**
- **Error codes seen (Developer report, Oct 2025, iOS 26):** `com.apple.healthkit Code=100 "Failed to send data to remote session."` and `Code=554 "Primary session is unreachable."`. An Apple DTS workaround for a 26.x mirroring failure: call `startMirroringToCompanionDevice` only after the session reaches `.prepared` or `.running`. [Forum 804276](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/804276)
- **"Remote session delegate is not set up" (Developer blog):** sends made before `prepare()` fail, and even afterwards "every now and then it fails to send data"; retrying helps. [Nonstrict](https://nonstrict.eu/blog/2024/hkworkoutsession-remote-delegate-not-setup-error/)
- **Hanging sends (Developer report, no Apple reply):** in about 5% of Workouts the send never completes, on watchOS 10/11 and iOS 17/18. Some calls throw "30+ minutes later". [Forum 769355](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/769355)
- **Slow end (Developer report, DTS asked for a feedback report):** `session.end()` can take "around a minute or two to actually fully end". Starting a new workout in that window desynchronises mirrored sessions. [Forum 772422](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/772422). This is relevant if the split is done by ending one session and starting another.
- **Ordering across a reconnect, and end-to-end delivery: undocumented.** Only the receive-side, suspended-app ordering above is documented.

## 5. Clocks

- **Apple:** the Watch face can be set up to 59 minutes ahead, but "Your alarms, notifications, World Clock, and other times will still match the actual time". So `Date()` is not affected. [Change the time settings on your Apple Watch](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105035)
- **Community (Apple Community "Level 10" user, not Apple staff):** "Apple Watch inherits its time directly from the paired iPhone", and "iPhone time is automatically set via Apple's time servers, which in turn sync with network time protocol (NTP) servers." The original poster saw iPhone and Watch agree with each other while both were 72 s off time.gov. [Apple Community 256123631](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256123631)
- **Unverified / not found:** any Apple document on the Watch's time source when it is away from the iPhone (Wi-Fi or cellular models), on how often it re-syncs, or on expected skew. Nothing found either on whether the `date` in a mirrored session's `didChangeTo` callback comes from the Watch's clock or the iPhone's.
- *Design inference:* device clocks are likely within about a second of each other when paired, but this is unmeasured. User-set manual time on the iPhone would carry over. Deciding "Set after T" by comparing two devices' timestamps near T can misclassify Sets. Prefer causal ordering, where each change records the last change it had seen from the other device, and use timestamps only as a tie-break with a tolerance. Optionally, estimate the offset on each reconnect with a timestamp ping-pong over the channel.

## Device tests to settle the open points
1. On the Watch, finish a session normally, then build a second workout with a plain `HKWorkoutBuilder` over a sub-window, adding the same already-saved HR and energy samples. Check `predicateForObjects(from:)` for both workouts, Health app totals, and the Move ring for double-counting.
2. Re-save the Watch workout with the same sync identifier, a higher version and an earlier end date. Is it replaced? Which samples stay associated? Repeat the save from the **iPhone** app (a different source): is it replaced, duplicated, or refused?
3. Check `sample.sourceRevision.source` of live HR and energy samples. Can the Watch app delete them with `deleteObjects(of:predicate:)`? Can the iPhone app?
4. Delete an `HKWorkout` only. Do its associated HR and energy samples remain?
5. Mirroring: walk the iPhone out of range. Log the Watch's send results during the gap, then check what the new iPhone session receives after reconnect. Then end the Watch session during a gap and confirm that the iPhone gets nothing further from HealthKit.
6. Log `Date()` on both devices in a ping-pong over the channel, across several reconnects and after airplane mode, to measure skew.
