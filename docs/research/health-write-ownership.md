# Who can write, change and delete our Workout in Apple Health

This note answers one architecture question: when the Watch has saved a Workout's `HKWorkout` and its heart-rate and active-energy samples, what can the **iPhone** app later do to that record? Three cases matter: the user edits start or end times on iPhone; a conflict review on iPhone Discards the Workout (delete the record and its samples) or changes its end; and a plain iPhone-saved `HKWorkout` has to be replaced by the Watch's full one. It also covers what the Watch can do in the background if the iPhone asks it to make the change.

Targets iOS 17 / watchOS 10 up to iOS / watchOS 27, with a **dependent** (companion) Watch app. Terms follow `CONTEXT.md` (**Workout**, **Live**, **Finished**, **Discard**, **Connected** / **Disconnected**). It builds on and does not repeat:
- [`healthkit-types-and-apis.md`](healthkit-types-and-apis.md): API availability, metadata keys, authorisation.
- [`watch-force-quit-and-hr-samples.md`](watch-force-quit-and-hr-samples.md): samples are saved *during* the session, and `discardWorkout` keeps them.
- [`workout-split-and-mirroring-channel.md`](workout-split-and-mirroring-channel.md) §2–3: rebuilding a workout from already-saved samples, and the delete rules.

Sources were read on 2026-09-24. Header quotes come from the iOS 26.0 and **iOS 27.0** SDK headers, read through the [xybp888/iOS-SDKs](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs) mirror. The text is Apple's, but the mirror is not an Apple site. The WWDC17 transcript is a third-party copy, because Apple no longer hosts the 2017 videos. Every claim is labelled **Apple** (docs, headers, WWDC, sample code), **Apple engineer** (a Frameworks Engineer or DTS reply on the Developer Forums), **Developer report**, or **Unverified / inference**.

## Answer

### Facts

1. **Only the source that saved an object can delete it or replace it.** *Apple:* "Your app can delete only those objects that it has previously saved to the HealthKit store." Header: "An application may only delete objects that it previously saved." *Apple, WWDC17 221* on sync identifiers: "it's important to note that this is **restricted to your source**. So, only your app can override samples that you inserted." It also says this "applies to any HK object. Such as an HKSample, or an HKWorkout." So both routes to changing a record, delete and higher-version replace, are limited to the saving **source**.
2. **Health data can't be edited in place.** *Apple:* "HealthKit objects are all immutable", and "Workouts are mostly immutable … However, you can continue to add samples to the workouts." *Apple, WWDC20 10184:* "When you edit a sample, you need to actually delete and add a new sample." An edit is always delete plus re-save, or a re-save with a higher sync version, which HealthKit turns into a delete plus save. No API in any SDK up to iOS 27 builds a workout from an existing one or changes one's dates. I diffed the iOS 26.0 and 27.0 headers: iOS 27 adds zones and `getEarliestAuthorizedSampleDate`, and changes nothing about ownership or editing.
3. **Whether the Watch app counts as "our app" to the iPhone depends on the project structure. For our structure it is probably a separate source (unverified).**
   - **Legacy WatchKit-*extension* projects:** Watch and iPhone shared one identity. *Apple:* `handleAuthorizationForExtension` "prompts the user to authorize both the app and its extensions". *Apple engineer* thread (2021): workouts saved by the watchOS app carried "the bundle identifier from the iPhone App". Apple's own WWDC17 example of sync identifiers replacing across devices is a "workout app on our phone and a WatchKit extension".
   - **Single-target Watch apps** (the only Xcode 14+ template; Apple's 2023 multidevice sample is one, with bundle ID `….watchkitapp`): *developer reports* say the Watch and iPhone apps get **separate permission entries** and behave "like we have 2 different app[s] reading and writing in 2 different HealthKit stores".
   - No Apple document states which `HKSource` a single-target Watch app's samples carry. **This is the #1 thing to test on a device.**
4. **If the sources are separate, the iPhone can't delete, shorten or replace anything the Watch saved,** and a same-`SyncIdentifier` save from the iPhone won't replace the Watch's `HKWorkout`. Given fact 1, the most likely result is a **second, duplicate workout** (inference, test it). That breaks ADR-0006's assumption that "editing a Finished Workout's times re-saves with a higher version and Health replaces the record" whenever the iPhone does the editing. ADR-0003's upgrade path (the Watch saves the full record, and the **iPhone deletes its own** plain one) is already correct, because each device deletes only its own record.
5. **Watch data reaches the iPhone late, and the iPhone app only sees what has synced.** *Apple (DTS):* "Data that exists on your Apple Watch but isn't synchronized to the paired iPhone is not available to your iOS app. The HealthKit store synchronization … is not real-time, and there is no API that can speed up the pace." *Developer report:* about 10–20 minutes for heart rate. *Apple:* each device has its own store, and "old data is periodically purged from Apple Watch". *Apple engineer:* the Watch holds roughly the last 7 days. **The Watch can't query, and probably can't delete, a workout older than its retention window** (unverified).
6. **Apple's multi-device pattern is: the Watch saves the workout, and the iPhone sends data to the Watch.**
   - *Apple, WWDC23 10023 and its sample:* "I'll end the primary session and save it on the Apple Watch by finishing the builder. After saving the workout sample on Apple Watch, it syncs to my other devices." In the sample, the iPhone sends water intake over the mirroring channel, and the **Watch** adds it to its builder. Only the Watch calls `finishWorkout`.
   - *Apple, WWDC25 322 (iOS 26):* "If you have a Watch app, be sure to start the workout there to get all available metrics."
   - *Apple, `HKWorkout` docs:* "Create and save workouts on the device that makes the most sense … typically the device processing the user's workout."
   - Apple gives **no guidance on editing or deleting afterwards**.
7. **Ring credit can't be fully undone.**
   - *Apple:* on iOS, "Workout objects automatically contribute to both the Move and Exercise rings. The Exercise ring increases by the workout's total duration", plus Stand hours. On watchOS, Exercise comes from the session's sensor data.
   - *Apple engineer:* "Exercise minutes for workouts saved on phone are awarded by the system and you cannot delete those."
   - So any record the iPhone saves adds Exercise minutes that can't be removed. That includes a replacement for the Watch's record (the tombstone idea).
8. **The Watch can be woken in the background to act on a request, but only briefly and not reliably.**
   - *Apple:* `transferUserInfo` is queued and delivered in order. It triggers a `WKWatchConnectivityRefreshBackgroundTask`, and "the system launches your app in the background". Background tasks get "a few seconds", and an uncompleted task burns the budget, which "results in a crash".
   - While the Watch is locked, HealthKit **reads** fail (`errorDatabaseInaccessible`), though saves are cached. A delete needs the object, or a predicate match, from the store, so a background delete on a locked Watch will probably fail (unverified).
   - No user tap is needed if the Watch is unlocked and in range. Otherwise the request waits until the Watch app next runs.

### Recommendation (ours, not Apple's)

- **First, run device test T1 (source identity).** If a single-target dependent Watch app and its iPhone app turn out to be **one source**, the simplest design works: **the iPhone owns every edit** (delete plus re-save, or a higher sync version) and needs no Watch round-trip. Everything below assumes the more likely result, **separate sources**.
- **Adopt design C, "the device that saved a record is the only one that edits it", run through a durable Health-change queue.** The Watch keeps saving the full record (Apple's pattern, and it is the only way to get Watch heart rate on iOS 17–25). The iPhone saves only plain records for Workouts the Watch was absent from. When the iPhone needs to change a Watch-saved record (time edit, end change, Discard), it sends an idempotent `HealthChange(workoutId, action, newStart/newEnd, version)` by `transferUserInfo`. The Watch applies it the next time it can: on a background WatchConnectivity wake if it is unlocked, otherwise on next launch. It applies it by (a) building the corrected workout with `HKWorkoutBuilder`, the same `SyncIdentifier` and a higher version, re-adding the already-saved samples inside the new window, and deleting its own samples outside it, or (b) for Discard, deleting its workout and its samples. The iPhone treats the Health copy as "pending update" until the Watch confirms back.
- **Accept, and tell the user about, the case the Watch can't reach:** a Watch-saved workout older than the Watch's retention window, or a Watch that is unpaired or replaced. There the iPhone can't change Health. Show "The Health copy of this Workout was recorded by your Apple Watch and can't be changed from iPhone. Edit or delete it in the Health app." **Don't** re-save an iPhone copy (design D): it duplicates the workout, and its Exercise minutes can't be removed.
- **Update ADR-0006**: the sync-identifier replace works only within one source. Make the "who edits" rule explicit alongside ADR-0003's "who writes" rule.

## 1. HealthKit source identity: Watch app vs iPhone app

- **Apple, what a source is:** `HKSource` "Represents the entity that created an object stored by HealthKit". `bundleIdentifier`: "For apps, this property holds the app's bundle identifier." `HKSource.default()`: "Returns a source object for the current app." `sourceRevision`: "The system automatically sets this property to represent the current version of the app that saved the object". It is set only at save time and can't be chosen by the app. [HKSource](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hksource), [bundleIdentifier](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hksource/bundleidentifier), [default()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hksource/default()), [HKObject.sourceRevision](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkobject/sourcerevision), [HKSource.h (26.0)](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKSource.h)
- **Apple, Platform Security:** stored records carry "metadata to track the provenance of each data record. This metadata includes an app identifier that identifies which app stored the record." [Protecting access to user's health data](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/protecting-access-to-users-health-data-sec88be9900f/web)
- **Legacy extension-based Watch apps (watchOS 2–6 era) shared the iPhone app's identity:**
  - **Apple:** `handleAuthorizationForExtension` "prompts the user to authorize **both the app and its extensions** for the types that the extension requested." [handleAuthorizationForExtension](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/handleauthorizationforextension(completion:))
  - **Apple engineer (Jun 2021):** a developer found that `HKSource.default()` on watchOS "is different than the source associated to the saved workouts (Which contains the bundle identifier from the iPhone App)". The Frameworks Engineer asked for a Feedback report and suggested filtering by source manually. [Forum 683224](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/683224). This is an extension-era project, where the watch-saved workouts were attributed to the **iPhone** app.
  - **Developer report (2015):** a watchOS 2 app was "listed under sources" in the iOS Health app, meaning the iPhone app's entry. [Forum 20936](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/20936)
- **Authorisation moved to the Watch in watchOS 6:** "In watchOS 6 and later, this method displays the permission form on Apple Watch, enabling independent HealthKit apps. In watchOS 5 and earlier, this method prompts the user to authorize the app on their paired iPhone." (**Apple**) [requestAuthorization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/requestauthorization(toshare:read:completion:))
- **Single-target Watch apps (Xcode 14+; ours):**
  - **Developer reports (2022–2023, no Apple reply):** "Both the iPhone app and watch app now display a dialog … In the Old WatchApp Project, if access was granted in either app, the granted access is synchronized." In Settings, "permission settings for both the iPhone app and watch app are displayed independently." Another developer: "it's like we have 2 different app reading and writing in 2 different HealthKit stores". [Forum 715238](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/715238)
  - **Apple sample:** Apple's multidevice sample is single-target. Its Watch app's bundle ID is `…MirroringWorkoutsSample.watchkitapp`, with `WKCompanionAppBundleIdentifier` set to the iOS app. [Building a multidevice workout app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/building-a-multidevice-workout-app)
  - **Inference / unverified:** each is its own app with its own bundle ID and its own permission entry, so samples saved by the Watch app most likely carry the Watch app's bundle ID as `HKSource`, separate from the iPhone app's. **No Apple document confirms or denies this.**
- **Dependent vs independent:** nothing documented. The permission and source split described above comes from the single-target *project structure*, and the reports concern **dependent** apps. `WKRunsIndependentlyOfCompanionApp` changes installation, not HealthKit identity (inference).
- **OS versions:** watchOS 6 (authorisation on the Watch) is below our floor. Nothing in iOS 17–27 changes source identity; the iOS 26.0 → 27.0 header diff shows no change to `HKSource`, `HKObject` or the delete methods.

## 2. What an app may delete, and whether "your app" includes the companion

- **Apple:** `delete(_:)`: "Your app can delete only those objects that it has previously saved to the HealthKit store. If the user revokes sharing permission, you can no longer delete the object." `deleteObjects(of:predicate:)`: "Deletes objects saved by this application that match the provided type and predicate … When deleting multiple objects, if any object cannot be deleted, none of them are deleted." Both end with: "Although your app can manage only the objects it created and saved, users can always delete any data through the Health app." [delete(_:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/delete(_:withcompletion:)-78l1m), [deleteObjects(of:predicate:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/deleteobjects(of:predicate:withcompletion:))
- **Apple (header):** "An application may only delete objects that it previously saved." [HKHealthStore.h (27.0)](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS27.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKHealthStore.h)
- **Does "your app" include the companion?** No Apple statement names the Watch companion. The unit is the *source* (§1). With a legacy extension it was the same source, so yes. With single-target apps it is probably separate, so the iPhone can't delete the Watch's objects and the Watch can't delete the iPhone's (**unverified**, test T2). *Developer blog (2015):* "An app cannot delete Health data recorded by another app." [Crunchy Bagel](https://crunchybagel.com/recording-workouts-in-healthkit/)
- **Immutability (Apple):** "HealthKit objects are all immutable. With a few exceptions (such as the object's source revision), the object's properties are set when the object is first created and they cannot change." [HKObject](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkobject). For workouts: "Workouts are mostly immutable … However, you can continue to add samples to the workouts." [HKWorkout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkout). *WWDC20 10184:* "When you edit a sample, you need to actually delete and add a new sample. If you don't, you could be saving duplicated samples." [WWDC20 10184](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10184/)
- **No builder from an existing workout (Apple, header):** `HKWorkoutBuilder` has only `initWithHealthStore:configuration:device:`. `addSamples:` "The samples will be saved to the database if they have not already been saved. The constraints of -[HKHealthStore saveObject:withCompletion:] apply". The only change allowed after the fact is adding samples or activities. [HKWorkoutBuilder.h (27.0)](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS27.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKWorkoutBuilder.h)
- **Deleting a workout doesn't document a cascade.** *Developer report:* deleting the `HKWorkout` removed Active Energy and Distance but not Exercise minutes. *Apple engineer:* "Exercise minutes for workouts saved on phone are awarded by the system and you cannot delete those. The user has to manually delete them in the Health app." [Forum 681755](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/681755)
- **Relating an effort score to someone else's workout:** a DTS engineer's working example relates effort to "an existing workout retrieved from my HealthKit store" with read and share access to `workoutType` and `workoutEffortScore`. Whether that workout may come from a *different source* is not stated (**unverified**, test T6). [Forum 763539](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/763539)

## 3. Sync identifier and version across sources

- **Apple (header, unchanged in 27.0):** `HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier` "Represents an identifier for an HKObject that is **set by its source** and is used by HealthKit to uniquely identify an object. … Saving a HKObject with a HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier will replace an existing HKObject with the same HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier value if the new HKObject has a greater HKMetadataKeySyncVersion. The new sample will be added to all HKWorkouts or HKCorrelations containing the replaced sample." [HKMetadata.h](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS26.0.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/HealthKit.framework/Headers/HKMetadata.h)
- **Apple (docs):** "When you save an HKObject with a sync identifier, the system looks for any existing objects with the same sync identifier … If the new object has a greater sync version, the system replaces the old object with the new one." [HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkmetadatakeysyncidentifier). The docs page doesn't mention source scope.
- **Apple, WWDC17 221 (the session that introduced the keys; third-party transcript copy):**
  - "If your new sample has a higher version, HealthKit will delete the original sample, and then save your new sample in its place. If you save a sample with a lower version, HealthKit will ignore this save."
  - "This metadata applies to any HK object. Such as an HKSample, or an HKWorkout. Further, it's important to note that **this is restricted to your source. So, only your app can override samples that you inserted.**"
  - The worked example is "a workout app on our phone and a **WatchKit extension**": the watch saves v1, and the phone later saves v2, which replaces it on every device.
  - [WWDC17 221 transcript](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2017/221/)
- **Apple, WWDC20 10184:** "If your remote server were also syncing data to your app on Watch and you try to save the sample again, HealthKit would see that the sample already exists and ignore it." [WWDC20 10184](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10184/). This is also extension-era.
- **What this means for us:** replacement is **scoped to the source**. Apple's cross-device examples worked because the Watch extension *was* the iPhone app's source. With a single-target Watch app that is a separate source (§1), an iPhone save with the Watch record's sync identifier and a higher version will **not** replace the Watch's `HKWorkout`. It will most likely be saved alongside it as a duplicate (**inference**; the alternative, a refused save, is also undocumented; test T3). Within one device's source, replacement of an `HKWorkout` is documented (WWDC17), but what happens to the old workout's *associated samples* is not. Re-add them explicitly (see [`workout-split-and-mirroring-channel.md`](workout-split-and-mirroring-channel.md) §2).

## 4. How Watch-saved data reaches the iPhone

- **Apple:** "iPhone, Apple Watch, and visionOS each have their own HealthKit store. … HealthKit automatically syncs data between these devices. To save space, old data is periodically purged from Apple Watch. Use `earliestPermittedSampleDate()` to determine the earliest samples available on Apple Watch." [About the HealthKit framework](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/about-the-healthkit-framework)
- **Apple (DTS, Feb 2025):** "Your HealthKit queries on iOS only return or observe what the iOS HealthKit store currently has. Data that exists on your Apple Watch but isn't synchronized to the paired iPhone is not available to your iOS app. The HealthKit store synchronization between an iPhone and its paired Apple Watch is not real-time, and there is no API that can speed up the pace. Some actions, like bringing a HealthKit-enabled app to the foreground, can trigger a synchronization, but that is completely up to the system." The suggested fixes are WatchConnectivity or workout mirroring. [Forum 774953](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/774953)
- **Developer report:** about 10 minutes for steps and 10–20 minutes for heart rate to reach the iPhone. [Forum 680361](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/680361)
- **Apple engineer (2021):** "The watch contains a subset of data from the phone (approx last 7 days)". [Forum 683224](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/683224)
- **As what source?** The synced object keeps the source it was saved with (its `sourceRevision` is set at save). So on the iPhone it appears with the Watch app as source, whichever that is (§1). *Inference from the docs, not stated for sync.*
- **Consequences:** (a) the iPhone can't reliably find the Watch's `HKWorkout` right after Finish, so our own WatchConnectivity message is how the iPhone learns that the Watch saved it and what its UUID is; (b) the Watch can act only on recent workouts, since it purges old data and a query older than `earliestPermittedSampleDate` returns nothing (**Apple**: "Attempts to query samples before this date return samples after this date" [earliestPermittedSampleDate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/earliestpermittedsampledate())). Whether a Watch-side `deleteObjects(of:predicate:)` can delete an object already purged from the Watch, with the delete still propagating to the iPhone, is **unverified** (T5).
- **Deletes propagate (Apple, for iCloud):** "if health data syncs over iCloud, deleting data on one device removes it from all synced devices". [Platform Security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/protecting-access-to-users-health-data-sec88be9900f/web). That deletes sync the same way over the Watch pairing, and how fast, is inferred, not documented.

## 5. Apple's multi-device workout pattern

- **Apple, WWDC23 10023:** "The primary session's delegate method didReceiveDataFromRemoteDevice is called with the packaged data which I'll unpack and save on my Apple Watch. And when I'm done cycling, I'll end the primary session and save it on the Apple Watch by finishing the builder. After saving the workout sample on Apple Watch, it syncs to my other devices". Also: "If your app needs it, use sync identifiers and version numbers to keep the data consistent across your server and users' devices." [WWDC23 10023](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10023/)
- **Apple sample code ("Building a multidevice workout app"):** only the watchOS code path owns an `HKLiveWorkoutBuilder` and calls `endCollection` / `finishWorkout`. The iPhone *sends* a water quantity over the mirroring channel, and the Watch's `handleReceivedData` turns it into an `HKQuantitySample` and `addSamples` it to **its** builder. In other words, the iPhone never writes Health data itself; it asks the Watch to. [Sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/building-a-multidevice-workout-app)
- **Apple, HKWorkout docs:** "Create and save workouts on the device that makes the most sense for your application—typically the device processing the user's workout." [HKWorkout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkout)
- **Apple, WWDC25 322 (iOS 26):** "If you have a Watch app, be sure to start the workout there to get all available metrics. Just call Start Watch App from the Health Store. And once you have, be sure to mirror the workout to the iPhone. And finally, always use the workout builder API to create and save a workout." [WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/)
- **Editing or deleting afterwards:** no Apple session, sample or doc covers changing a multi-device workout after it is saved. **Not found.**

## 7. What the Watch app can do in the background when the iPhone asks

- **Delivery (Apple):** `transferUserInfo` "Call this method when you want to send a dictionary of data to the counterpart and ensure that it's delivered. Dictionaries sent using this method are queued on the other device and delivered in the order in which they were sent." It isn't supported in the Simulator. [transferUserInfo](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/transferuserinfo(_:))
- **Wake (Apple):** "Background watch connectivity tasks are triggered whenever the paired device sends data using" `updateApplicationContext`, `transferUserInfo`, `transferCurrentComplicationUserInfo` or `transferFile`. "The system launches your app in the background". Defer `setTaskCompleted()` "until after you've activated your session and received all the pending data." [WKWatchConnectivityRefreshBackgroundTask](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkwatchconnectivityrefreshbackgroundtask)
- **Budget (Apple):** "The system gives your app a few seconds of background execution time". "An uncompleted task consumes the background-task time budget … which results in a crash when the budget runs out." App refresh is "approximately four tasks per hour for each app with a complication on the active watch face". [Using background tasks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-background-tasks), [Transferring data with Watch Connectivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/transferring-data-with-watch-connectivity), [WKApplicationRefreshBackgroundTask](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkapplicationrefreshbackgroundtask)
- **Locked device (Apple):** "the device encrypts the HealthKit store when the user locks the device … your app may not be able to read data … However, your app can still write". `errorDatabaseInaccessible`: "This error occurs when your app queries for HealthKit data while the device is locked. You can, however, still save data." [Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy), [errorDatabaseInaccessible](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkerror/code/errordatabaseinaccessible). *Inference:* a rebuild needs a query for the samples, and a delete needs a query or predicate match, so both are likely to fail while the Watch is off-wrist or locked. They should be retried on the next unlock or launch.
- **Can't start a session in the background:** watchOS 10+ refuses to start or prepare a workout session from the background (see [`automatic-watch-join.md`](automatic-watch-join.md) §3). That doesn't matter here: an edit uses a plain `HKWorkoutBuilder` and deletes, not a session (inference).
- **Does the user need to open the app?** Not if the Watch is in range and unlocked: the background wake is enough, within seconds of work (**Apple** for the wake; unverified for HealthKit writes during it). Otherwise the change stays queued until the Watch app next runs. So "eventually, usually soon" is the honest promise.

## 8. Viable designs

| Design | How it works | Pros | Cons / risks | Documented vs unverified |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Watch owns all Health writes** | Every save and edit happens on the Watch. The iPhone only sends instructions. | Matches Apple's sample. One source, so sync-identifier replace and deletes always work. | Workouts with no Watch (iPhone-only) have no writer, so it degrades to C anyway. Edits wait for the Watch. Workouts older than the Watch's ~7-day window can't be edited. Doesn't work for users with no Watch. | Pattern documented. Background execution and retention limits documented. The edit round-trip is unverified. |
| **B. iPhone owns all Health writes** | The Watch runs the session for sensors but calls `discardWorkout()`. The iPhone later builds the `HKWorkout` with `HKWorkoutBuilder`, adding the Watch's already-saved HR and energy samples. | The iPhone can edit and delete the `HKWorkout` any time. | HR and energy samples are **still saved by the Watch source during the session** (Apple), so Discard can't delete them from the iPhone. Adding another source's samples to our workout is unverified. The iPhone must wait for Health sync (minutes) before it can build. An iOS-saved workout gets Exercise ring = duration, **plus** whatever the Watch session earned, and those minutes can't be deleted. Goes against Apple's advice. | Mostly unverified; several documented negatives. |
| **B′. iPhone live session (iOS 26+)** | The iPhone is primary and the Watch is not in a session. | iPhone owns everything. | No Watch heart rate (no HR sensor on iPhone). Not available on iOS 17–25. Mirroring can't go iPhone → Watch. | Documented. Fails the product's heart-rate goal. |
| **C. The device that saved a record edits it (recommended)** | The Watch edits Watch records and the iPhone edits iPhone records. The iPhone sends edits for Watch records through a durable queue (§7), and the Watch confirms back. | Uses only documented permissions. Keeps Watch heart rate. Plain→full upgrade already fits ADR-0003 (the iPhone deletes its own plain record). | Edits of Watch records are asynchronous and can fail permanently (old workout, Watch gone). Needs "pending Health update" state and a user-facing fallback. | Rules documented. Round-trip timing and locked-Watch behaviour unverified. |
| **D. Tombstone + re-save under the iPhone source** | The iPhone can't delete the Watch record, so it saves a corrected iPhone copy, perhaps asking the user to delete the Watch one in Health. | Instant, no Watch needed. | **Duplicate workouts** in Health and Fitness until the user cleans up. A sync-identifier save won't replace across sources (WWDC17). The extra iOS workout adds Exercise minutes (and Stand hours) that the app can't remove (Apple engineer). Discard can't be done at all. | The downsides are documented; the duplicate behaviour itself needs a test. |
| **E. Same-source by project structure** | Use a legacy WatchKit-extension project, where Watch data was attributed to the iPhone app. | Would make the iPhone the owner of everything. | Xcode 14+ templates are single-target. Whether an extension-based app still builds and ships on watchOS 10–27 wasn't checked (**unverified**). Relies on legacy behaviour Apple never documented as a feature. | Not recommended. |

**Choice:** C, which becomes "A for Watch-saved records, iPhone for iPhone-saved records". If T1 shows that one source covers both apps, switch to "the iPhone edits everything" and drop the queue.

## Device tests to settle the unknowns

Run each on the oldest (iOS 17 / watchOS 10) and newest (iOS / watchOS 27) supported pair, with a single-target dependent Watch app like ours.

1. **T1, source identity (decision gate).** Save a workout and HR samples on the Watch and a workout on the iPhone. On the iPhone, log `sourceRevision.source.bundleIdentifier` for each, compare them with `HKSource.default()` on each device, and see what the Health app's Sources list shows. Also check the source of the live HR and energy samples the Watch session collected.
2. **T2, cross-device delete.** From the iPhone app, `delete` the Watch-saved `HKWorkout` and `deleteObjects(of: heartRate, predicate: …)` for the Watch's samples. Record the error code, or the success and the deleted count. Then repeat from the Watch against an iPhone-saved workout.
3. **T3, cross-source sync identifier.** The Watch saves a workout with `SyncIdentifier = X`, v1. The iPhone saves one with `X`, v2 and a shorter end. Result: replaced, duplicated, or refused? Repeat both within one device (same source) and check whether associated samples carry over or must be re-added.
4. **T4, rings.** After T3's duplicate case (and a same-source replace), note the Exercise, Move and Stand values before and after. Then delete the iPhone copy: which ring values stay?
5. **T5, Watch retention.** On the Watch, query and delete a Watch-saved workout that is 3, 8 and 30 days old. Check `earliestPermittedSampleDate()` on the Watch, and whether the delete reaches the iPhone store and how long it takes.
6. **T6, effort on another source's workout.** From the iPhone (iOS 18+), `relateWorkoutEffortSample` against a Watch-saved workout.
7. **T7, background edit.** With the Watch app suspended and the Watch on wrist and unlocked, `transferUserInfo` a Discard. Measure the time until the delete appears on the iPhone. Repeat with the Watch off-wrist and locked (expect `errorDatabaseInaccessible`), then put it back on and confirm the retry works.
8. **T8, sync latency.** Time from the Watch's `finishWorkout()` to the iPhone's `HKObserverQuery` / anchored query seeing the workout, with the iPhone app in the foreground and in the background.
